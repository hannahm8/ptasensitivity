\section{Method}

Following the method in Ref.~\cite{SiemensEtAl:2013}. 
The gravitational wave spectrum is given by 
\begin{equation}
\Pg (f) = \frac{A^2}{24 \pi^2} \left(\frac{f}{\fref}\right)^{2\alpha} f^{-3}\,,
\label{eqn:Pgw}
% equation 16 in SiemansEtAl:2013
\end{equation}
where $f$ is the gravitational wave frequency, $\fref$ is a reference frequency chosen as $1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, $A$ is the amplitude of the background at $f=\fref$, $\alpha$ is the slope of the spectrum, and $\fref$ is a reference frequency chosen as $1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$. 
For a gravitational wave background signal and a given pulsar timing array with timespan $T$, the average signal to noise ratio (SNR) is given by
\begin{equation}
\left< \rho \right> = \left( 2 T \sum_{IJ} \chiIJhd^2 
                             \int_{f_L}^{f_H} {\rm d}f  
                             \frac{\Pg^2 (f)}{\PI(f)\PJ(f)}
                            \right)^{1/2} \,,
\label{eqn:snrPIPJPg}
\end{equation}
where $\sum_{IJ}$ is the sum over all pulsar pairs ($I \neq J$) and $P_I$ is the pwer spectrum of the $I{\rm th}$ pulsar. 
The integration limits are given by $f_L=1/T$ and $f_H=c/2$ where $c$ is the cadence of observations (assumed to be the same for all pulsars). 
The Hellings and Downs coefficients $\chi_{IJ}$ for pulsars separated by angle $\zeta$ are given by
\begin{equation}
\chiIJhd = \frac{1}{2} 
           - \frac{1}{4} \left(\frac{1 - \cos(\zeta)}{2}\right)
           + \frac{3}{2} \left(\frac{1 - \cos(\zeta)}{2}\right) 
           \log \left( \frac{1 - \cos(\zeta)}{2} \right)\,.
\label{eqn:hd}
\end{equation}
If we assume evenly sampled data and that there is only white noise in the pulsars then $\PI$ is the sum of Eqn.~\ref{eqn:Pgw} and the white noise, 
\begin{equation}
\PI (f) = \frac{A^2}{24 \pi^2} \left(\frac{f}{\fref}\right)^{2\alpha} f^{-3} 
          + 2 \sigma_I^2 \Delta t\,,
\end{equation}
where $c=1/\Delta t$ and $\sigma_I$ is the timing precision for the $I{\rm th}$ pulsar. 
Pulsars may also contain spin noise or red noise. 
The power spectral density of spin noise for the $I{\rm th}$ pulsar is given by 
\begin{equation}
% from Goncharov 2021 eqn 5
\PsnI (f) = \frac{\ARedI^2}{12 \pi^2} 
            \left( \frac{f}{\fref} \right)^{-\gammaRedI} {\rm yr}^3\,,
\end{equation}
where $\ARedI$ and $\gammaRedI$ are the amplitude and slope of the power law for the $I$th pulsar and ${\rm yr}$ is the number of seconds in a year. 

Jitter noise $\sigJitter$ has also been measured in several of the pulsars. 
This modifies the white noise part of the spectrum. 

Including red noise and jitter noise, $\PI(f)$ is then 
\begin{equation}
\PI (f) = \frac{A^2}{24 \pi^2} \left(\frac{f}{\fref}\right)^{2\alpha} f^{-3} 
          ~+~ 2 \left( \sigma_I^2 + \sigma_{{\rm jit}, I}^2 \right) \Delta t 
          ~+~ \frac{\ARedI^2}{12 \pi^2} 
            \left( \frac{f}{\fref} \right)^{-\gammaRedI} {\rm yr}^3\,.
\label{eqn:PIGWWR}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\sigma = \delta / \sqrt{T_{\rm obs}}\,,

where $\delta$ is a constant of proportionality which depends on the pulsar.

Can we re-organise how the available time is split between pulsars to increase the SNR at$10\,{\rm yr}$?
Shuffle time between pulsar:
\begin{itemize}
\item Choose a pulsar to be the donor pulsar. Subtract $1/4$ of its integration time. 
\item Give time to each other pulsar in turn and compute whether there is an SNR improvement.
\item Repeat for next donor pulsar. 
\end{itemize}

Eqn.~\ref{eqn:hd} has highest values for pulsars that have small angular separations.
But we want good sky coverage to confirm a Hellings-Downs correlation for detection. 
To prevent the time-shuffle algorithm favouring pulsars in the galactic centre, we replace the Hellings and Downs correlation $\chiIJhd$ with a modified angular correlation function $\chi_{IJ}^{\rm mod}$ which corresponds to the difference between a dipole $\chiIJdip$ and the HD correlation
\begin{equation}
\chi_{IJ}^{\rm mod} = \chiIJdip - 2 \chiIJhd
\label{eqn:chimod}
\end{equation}
and $\chiIJdip$ is given by~\cite{AnholmEtAl:2009}
\begin{equation}
\chiIJdip = -\frac{3}{2}
            \left[ \cos \zeta - \frac{4}{3} - 4 \tan^2 \frac{\zeta}{2} 
                   \ln \left( \sin \frac{\zeta}{2} \right) \right]\,.
\end{equation}
The correlation functions are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:correlations}.
