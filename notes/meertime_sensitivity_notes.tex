\title{Optimising Meertime for PTA}

\maketitle

Following the method in Ref.~\cite{SiemensEtAl:2013}. 
The gravitational wave sepctrum is given by 
\begin{equation}
\Pg (f) = \frac{A^2}{24 \pi^2} \left(\frac{f}{\fref}\right)^{2\alpha} f^{-3}\,,
\label{eqn:Pgw}
% equation 16 in SiemansEtAl:2013
\end{equation}
where $f$ is the gravitational wave frequency, $A$ is the amplitude of the background, $\alpha$ is the slope of the spectrum, and $\fref$ is a reference frequency chosen as $1\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$. 
For a gravitatoinal wave background signal and a given pulsar timing array with timespan $T$, the average signal to noise ratio (SNR) is given by
\begin{equation}
\left< \rho \right> = \left( 2 T \sum_{IJ} \chi_{IJ}^2 
                             \int_{f_L}^{f_H} {\rm d}f  
                             \frac{\Pg^2 (f)}{\PI(f)\PJ(f)}
                            \right)^{1/2} \,,
\label{eqn:snrPIPJPg}
\end{equation}
where $\sum_{IJ}$ is the sum over all pulsar pairs ($I \neq J$) and $P_I$ is the pwer spectrum of the $I{\rm th}$ pulsar. 
The integration limits are given by $f_L=1/T$ and $f_H=c/2$ where $c$ is the cadence of observations (assumed to be the same for all pulsars). 
The Hellings and Downs coefficients $\chi_{IJ}$ for pulsars separated by angle $\zeta$ are given by
\begin{equation}
\chi_{IJ} = \frac{1}{2} 
            - \frac{1}{4} \left(\frac{1 - \cos(\zeta)}{2}\right)
            + \frac{3}{2} \left(\frac{1 - \cos(\zeta)}{2}\right) 
            \log \left( \frac{1 - \cos(\zeta)}{2} \right)\,.
\end{equation}
If we assume evenly sampled data and that there is only white noise in the pulsars then $\PI$ is the sum of Eqn.~\ref{eqn:Pgw} and the white noise, 
\begin{equation}
\PI (f) = \frac{A^2}{24 \pi^2} \left(\frac{f}{\fref}\right)^{2\alpha} f^{-3} 
          + 2 \sigma_I^2 \Delta t\,,
\end{equation}
where $c=1/\Delta t$ and $\sigma_I$ is the timing precision for the $I{\rm th}$ pulsar. 
Pulsars may also contain spin noise or red noise. 
The power spectral density of spin noise for the $I{\rm th}$ pulsar is given by 
\begin{equation}
% from Goncharov 2021 eqn 5
\PsnI (f) = \frac{\ARedI^2}{12 \pi^2} 
            \left( \frac{f}{\fref} \right)^{-\gammaRedI} {\rm yr}^3\,,
\end{equation}
where $\ARedI$ and $\gammaRedI$ are the amplitude and slope of the power law for the $I$th pulsar and ${\rm yr}$ is the number of seconds in a year. 
$\PI(f)$ is then 
\begin{equation}
\PI (f) = \frac{A^2}{24 \pi^2} \left(\frac{f}{\fref}\right)^{2\alpha} f^{-3} 
          ~+~ 2 \sigma_I^2 \Delta t 
          ~+~ \frac{\ARedI^2}{12 \pi^2} 
            \left( \frac{f}{\fref} \right)^{-\gammaRedI} {\rm yr}^3\,.
\end{equation}

\section{Data}

MeerTime is timing $82$ pulsars~\cite{MeerTimeSpreadsheet} targetting a precision of $1\times 10^{-6}\,{\rm s}$ for each with a $c=26$ per year.
From Ref.~\cite{MeerTimeSpreadsheet} we have an estimated precision and the integration time required to achieve it. 
Precision $\sigma$ scales with observation $T_{\rm obs}$ time like 
\begin{equation}
\sigma = \delta / \sqrt{T_{\rm obs}}\,,
\end{equation}
where $\delta$ is a constant of proportionality which depends on the pulsar. 


Some pulsars have a previous noise analysis by the PPTA and NANOGrave. 
Currently we don't have enough data to make an estimate for the red noise parameters with MeerTime. 
We take $\ARed$ and $\gammaRed$ values from Refs.~\cite{GoncharovEtAl:2021} (sixth and seventh column in Table 1) and ~\cite{NANOGrav12.5yr:2021} (tenth and eleventh column in Table 5). 
Where a value is listed in both papers, we choose the Ref.~\cite{GoncharovEtAl:2021} value.

We set $\ARed=0$ for all other pulsars.
