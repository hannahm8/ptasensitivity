
The MeerTime PTA is capable of timing large numbers of pulsars to high precision. 
Current observing strategies predict promising results for the first few years of data, assuming a SGWB signal consistent with the spectulative detection from PTA results. 
Our investigation into whether small adjustments to the observing schedule assumes a simiplified PTA, with constant cadence and minimal variation in anticipated precision, however it can provide useful insights into where gains can be made. 
Using the current allocated observing time of $12\,{\rm hrs}$ per two-week block, we find a possible gain of $\approx 6\%$ in S/N. 
We also find that if the observing time allocation for MeerTime is increased by $20\%$, up to $12\%$ (from S/N $12.3$ to $13.8$) through a combination of the additional observing time and the time-swap analysis presented here.
